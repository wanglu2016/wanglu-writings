前一阵，我在《华林》（Hualin International Journal of Buddhist Studies ）上看到一篇写阿毗达磨的文章。我平常的习惯是，看到文章有写错的地方，记下来，直接发在豆瓣或公号上。不过，那篇文章里，作者的一个重要观点是有问题的，邮箱又在PDF的开头，我就给作者发了邮件，举了5个问题。作者认可了细节问题，但对于重要观点（第1条），他说不确定引文能反驳他的观点，又把他最近（2023）发表在Religious Studies 上的文章发给我，问我是否看过。
我读了一遍，发现仍然存在问题，就列了6处发给他。有些是细节，有些牵涉到论文的核心结论。这可能让他有点不愉快。他说，第一条，也许你是对的，但你也不能否认我就不对；第三到五条，我不认为我们的观点有什么不同，除了一些小细节，你提的就像我文章的摘要；第六条，我不明白你的逻辑。第六条是牵涉到他结论的一条。由于我之前已经附上了相关文本，他说不明白，我就又把文本（《顺正理论》《显宗论》）简单翻译成英文解释了一遍。他没有再表态。
现在，我把学术讨论的部分发出来（略去了称呼和寒暄）。以下三封，都节选自我发给他的邮件。第一封是关于他《华林》（2020）那篇；后两封是关于他Religious Studies（2023）那篇。两篇网上都不难搜到。

邮件二：
The question mentioned in the last email, my answer is: yes. For those who have come to the form realm, the five aggregates are all belonging to the form realm. "欲界法滅者謂欲界死有諸蘊。色界法現在前者謂色界中有諸蘊"; "various" (諸) of "various aggregates" (諸蘊), indicates that it is not only one kind of aggregate. Or, I can also quote another paragraph to try to explain.
《阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論》卷53：「有結在欲界彼結非[1]墮欲界。謂纏所纏欲界沒起色界中有。欲界沒生色界者。通異生及聖者。彼色界中有在欲界起法應如是。死有滅處中有現前。如種滅處萌芽現前。彼從死有至中有時。若異生色界三十一隨眠隨一現前令生相續。若聖者色界修所斷三隨眠隨一現前令生相續。」(CBETA 2023.Q1, T27, no. 1545, p. 273a8-14)[1] 墮【大】，隋【宋】【元】【明】，隨【宮】
Here, the "thirty-one afflictions of the form realm" (色界三十一隨眠) belongs to the "saṃskāraskandha" (行蘊), they are mental factors (心所法). Because these mental factors correspond (相應) to the "mind" and correspond to the "sensation" (受心所) and "perception" (想心所), then, the consciousness aggregate (識蘊), sensation aggregate (受蘊) and perception aggregate (想蘊) are also belonging to the form realm. Therefore, for antarābhava in the form realm, the five aggregates in the continuum (相續) are all belonging to the form realm.

1.
P13：Saṅghabhadra maintains that plants and other insentient beings – whether living or not – are composed of only one aggregate (that of bodily form), whereas only sentient beings are composed of all five. As Saṅghabhadra’s avers in his Nyāyānusāra: ‘Insentient beings are constituted in only one aggregate (i.e. rūpaskandha)’ (無有情唯具一蘊) (T1562.29.402c11).
It seems that you understand "無" and "有情" together as "Insentient beings" (無有情, 非情). For "無有情唯具一蘊", my understanding is this: there are no sentient beings with only one skandha. "無" denies "有情唯具一蘊". The following "亦無有情唯具三蘊" is the same understanding: there are no sentient beings with only three skandhas. In other words, sentient beings can have five skandhas (born in the kāmadhātu and rūpadhātu), four skandhas (born in the ārūpyadhātu), or two skandhas (when entering nirodha-samāpatti 滅受想定).
《阿毘達磨順正理論》卷12：「故無有情唯具一蘊。又心心所不相離故，亦無有情唯具三蘊。」(CBETA 2023.Q1, T29, no. 1562, p. 402c11-12)
2.
P13：In his Abhidharmakośabhāṣya, Vasubandhu avers that there is no antarābhava undergone by sentient beings who perish and immediately become reborn within the formless realm (T1558.29.46a18; see also 3.37d).
It seems that you think no antarābhava requires two conditions to be met: 1. Perish within the formless realm; 2. Reborn within the formless realm. In my view, only the latter condition needs to be met: reborn within the formless realm. That is to say, if a sentient being in the kāmadhātu or rūpadhātu dies and is reborn in the ārūpyadhātu, it is also no antarābhava ("謂欲色界歿生無色界唯生生有"). As for the sentient beings who die in the ārūpyadhātu and are reborn in the kāmadhātu or rūpadhātu, there is antarābhava. You have already cited evidence for the latter point. There is another passage that can prove both points at the same time:
《阿毘達磨大毘婆沙論》卷68：「無色界歿生欲界者。謂生中有。此唯異生。此中非在色界死者。謂無色界死有在無色界滅故。非在色界生者。謂欲界中有在欲界起故。非受色有者謂受欲有。即受欲界中有諸蘊。諸在無色界死生者皆受無色有耶。答諸在無色界死生者皆受無色有。謂無色界無諸色故。無下中有在彼起義。故不可說在彼死生不受彼有。此通異生及諸聖者。異生生上亦生下一一處容受多生。聖者生上不生下一一處唯受一生。有受無色有非在無色界死而[4]在無色界生。謂欲色界歿生無色界唯生生有。此通異生及諸聖者。此中受無色有者謂受無色界生有諸蘊。非在無色界死者。謂欲色界死有在欲色界滅故。而在無色界生者。謂無色界生有在無色界起故。」(CBETA 2023.Q1, T27, no. 1545, p. 353c1-18)[4] 在【大】，生【宋】【元】【明】【宮】

P9：He reasons that, because the stuff making up the reflection in the mirror and the mirror are ‘not equiva- lent’ (Skt. asādharmya; Chi. budeng 不等) in both being ‘actual entities’ (Skt. bhāva),……

Here, it seems that you understand "not equivalent" as "the reflection" and "the mirror" not equivalent; and believe that the sentence "謂別色生說名為像，其體實有理所不成" also explains "又非等故為喻不成". This is inconsistent with my understanding. According to my understanding, not equivalent refers to "the relationship between the object and the reflection of the object" and "the relationship between death (死有) and rebirth (生有)" not equivalent, that is, the two relationships are not similar. The reason for "not equivalent" is: the former "is not a continuum" (非一相續), the latter "is a continuum" (是一相續). The sentence "謂別色生說名為像" explains "像實有不成", not "不等故非譬". "The reflection does not actually exist as entity" (像實有不成) and "not equivalent, so the two relationships are not similar" (不等故非譬) are two views of Vasubandhu. Vasubandhu expounded these two views in succession. "謂別色生說名為像" only explains the first view. P14：The state of becoming reborn does not include [a gross physical support in the intermediate being], whereas the two physical conditions only arise in the state of becoming deceased, which lacks a distinctly predominant physical basis; therefore, the example cited [by the opponent] is not equivalent to the target property [of the state of becoming reborn]. Nor can one say that apart from the semen and blood, which are insentient, there is a distinctly predominant physical basis as the undergirding condition [of the state of becoming reborn]. Since upapādukas are spontaneously generated in space, which one of these two conditions serves as the predominant basis?
This passage is a translation of the following content: "生有無容由二緣起，唯有死有，無別勝依，故所引喻，非等於法。亦不可說以外非情精血等緣為勝依性，由化生者空中欻生，於中計何為勝依性?"
I don't think this translation is quite appropriate. I understand the original text to mean: the generation of an object's reflection requires two conditions: 1. The object itself; 2. A mirror or something similar to a mirror. However, the generation of "becoming reborn" (生有) has only one condition: becoming deceased (死有). Therefore, the relationship between "becoming deceased and becoming reborn" is not similar to the relationship between an object and its reflection. Some may say that the generation of "becoming reborn" also has a second condition: 1. Becoming deceased; 2. Semen and blood. But this view is inappropriate, because for upapādukas, there is no semen and blood.
5.
P10：Vasubandhu and Saṅghabhadra accept that the concept of interstitial space is required to explain two conventional aspects about ‘a reflection appearing in a mirror’. The first is that the reflection and the mirror are two separate entities; the second is that the reflec- tion and the mirror do not occupy the same physical place.
Here, I also have different views. I believe Vasubandhu did not accept the first one. He did not believe the reflection is an entity (實有). In fact, there was no dispute about whether the mirror and the object itself were entities. The dispute was only that Saṅghabhadra believed the reflection was an entity, while Vasubandhu believed it was not. As for the second point, whether the mirror and the reflection occupy the same physical place, Vasubandhu believed they did. This was an important basis for Vasubandhu to prove that the reflection was not an entity. Saṅghabhadra, on the contrary, believed they did not occupy the same physical place. We can also see from the Samayapradīpikā that Saṅghabhadra believed Vasubandhu insisted that the mirror and the reflection occupy the same physical place.

The reason why the reflection analogy is inappropriate is not because the reflection has no entity, but because of the following reasons:
1. The reflection will show the same changes as the things change, but "birth" will not change like "death". Specifically, when an object breaks, the reflection also breaks with it; but if "birth" breaks when "death" breaks, the continuity of life will not be established.
2. The reflection and the object are similar, the reflection of the moon is still the shape of the moon, but "birth" and "death" are not similar, otherwise, when a cow dies, it cannot be reborn as a human, it must still be a cow. But this is obviously absurd.
3. An object can produce multiple reflections, but from one "death," multiple "births" cannot be produced.
4. The object and the reflection must appear at the same time, but "death" and "birth" must not be simultaneous.
Therefore, it is appropriate to use grains as an analogy, and it is inappropriate to use reflection as an analogy.
